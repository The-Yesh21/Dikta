Executive Solution Pitch

Dikta

Notes read aloud at the rhythm of handwriting. A mobile dictation companion for students copying notes by hand.

1. The Problem

Students retain up to 40% more conceptual depth when copying study material by hand. But copying from screens or textbooks causes continual neck fatigue, visual context switching, and fragmented focus.

Traditional audiobooks and Text-to-Speech (TTS) tools read continuously at 130–170 WPM. Human handwriting averages only 25–40 WPM. When copying, students are forced to pause and rewind every three seconds. Tools built for listening fail completely for writing.

2. The Solution: Dikta

Dikta dictates notes in short 3–8 word phrases, followed by mathematically calculated writing pauses dynamically calibrated to the student's physical handwriting cadence.

writeSeconds = (graphemes / userLpm) * 60 * scriptFactor
gap = max(minGap, writeSeconds - 0.5 * speechSeconds) + boundaryPause

3. Core Differentiators

Grapheme Pacing Engine

Uses Dart characters for true grapheme clusters (Latin, Devanagari, Kannada), fatigue decay ($e^{-t / \tau}$), and an adaptive feedback loop ($\alpha = 0.25 \to 0.05$).

Teacher's Syllable Spelling

Technical words (≥10 letters) are spelled naturally: "M, I, T, O · mito · C, H, O, N, D, R, I, A · mitochondria."

PDF Headings & Diagrams

Detects headings by font size (≥1.15× body) and image ratios to tell students: "Leave ~8 lines of space to draw diagram later."

Trilingual Audio Parity

Native timbre voices for English (en-IN), Hindi (hi-IN), and Kannada (kn-IN) via Gnani Vachana AI with leading silence buffers.
